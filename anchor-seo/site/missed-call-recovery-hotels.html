What a missed call actually costs

Run your own numbers with this worksheet:

  1. Missed calls per day. Pull it from your phone system or carrier portal — most show unanswered/abandoned calls. No log access? Run a one-week tally sheet at the desk, or mystery-call your own property at 8am, 4pm, and 11pm for a week and record how often a human answers.
  2. Booking intent rate. What share of inbound calls are booking-related? For limited-service properties, availability/rate calls plus group inquiries are typically the largest slice of inbound volume. Use your own desk's estimate.
  3. Conversion if answered. A live answer on a high-intent call books a meaningful share of the time; voicemail books almost never. Use a conservative answered-call conversion figure from your own experience.
  4. Value per booking. ADR × average length of stay — and remember one in some number of "calls" is an 18-room block, not a single room-night.

Missed calls/day × booking-intent % × conversion % × booking value × 365. Even with conservative inputs, a 60–100 room property usually lands on a five-figure annual number. With one captured group block included, it climbs fast. The estimate is directional, not a guarantee — but "directionally five figures" is enough to act on.

Where calls get missed

The leak concentrates in four predictable places:

  • Overnight and early morning. No desk coverage, or an auditor buried in the audit. The same-night booker and the late-planning coach both land here. (Deep dive: overnight hotel call answering.)
  • The rush hours. Check-in surge at 3–6pm, checkout at 8–11am. One agent, a line of guests, a ringing phone — the in-person guest wins, the caller dials the next property.
  • Shift gaps and breaks. The 20 minutes nobody talks about: lunch, bathroom, walking a guest to the ice machine, dealing with a housekeeping issue on the third floor.
  • Hold and abandon. Technically answered, practically missed: callers parked on hold quietly hang up and don't call back.

Recovery discipline for stragglers is simple and worth writing down: every voicemail and missed-call number gets a callback within 15 minutes during staffed hours. Missed call recovery FAQs

How do I find my missed-call count without new software?

Three options: your phone system or carrier portal's unanswered-call report; a one-week paper tally at the desk; or a structured mystery-call test of your own property across mornings, rush hours, and late nights.

Is this just call forwarding to an answering service?

Forwarding is the plumbing; recovery is what happens next. Generic services take messages — which is voicemail with better handwriting. Recovery means the call is answered by an agent that can resolve FAQs, guide on rates, capture complete booking details, and escalate correctly.

Can recovered calls turn into bookings if Ava doesn't confirm the reservation herself?

Yes — because the alternative wasn't "Ava books it or your team books it." The alternative was voicemail. A captured lead with a fast callback converts; an abandoned call converts at roughly zero. And your team keeps control of final rates and inventory.

What's the biggest source of missed-call revenue?

For most limited-service properties: after-hours booking calls, with group inquiries as the highest-value individual misses. One captured tournament or crew block can cover months of service cost.

How quickly will I see results?
