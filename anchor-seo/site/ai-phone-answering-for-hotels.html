What hotel-specific AI means

The difference shows up in triage. An inbound hotel line carries new bookings, group inquiries, current guests, tomorrow's arrivals, vendors, and the occasional emergency — all on one number. A generic answering AI flattens those into messages. A hotel-specific agent handles each lane differently: FAQs answered to completion from your factsheet, booking inquiries captured with dates, party size, and contact details, group leads routed to whoever runs your sales, and urgent calls transferred live.

It also shows up in the vocabulary. Ava understands ADR-adjacent questions ("what do rooms start at this weekend?"), late check-in logistics, pet fees, crew lodging, and the difference between a wedding block and a single king room — without your desk writing a single script.

Guardrails: what Ava will never do

The fastest way to lose trust in automation is letting it overpromise. Ava operates inside hard limits you approve:

  • No invented rates. She gives approved starting-rate guidance — "rooms start at $110, depending on availability" — and routes exact quotes to your team.
  • No unauthorized confirmations. She captures complete booking requests; your team confirms. You keep control of inventory and final numbers.
  • No improvising on safety. Emergencies, lockouts, and in-stay issues transfer immediately by rules you set and test.
  • No pretending. Asked if she's an AI, she says so.
