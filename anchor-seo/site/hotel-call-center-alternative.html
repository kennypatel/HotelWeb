Call center vs. answering service vs. AI agent

The outsourced call center staffs humans on your line and bills accordingly: per minute or per seat, with minimums. Quality depends on whoever picks up, training fades between refreshes, and the meter runs fastest in exactly the months you can least see straight.

The generic answering service is cheaper because it does less: it takes messages. For perishable hotel calls — the same-night booking, the tournament block — a message read at 7am is a booking your competitor already checked in.

The hotel-trained AI agent answers instantly on every ring, never has a staffing gap, resolves routine calls to completion, captures revenue calls in structured detail, and transfers urgent calls to your own on-call staff. The cost is flat. The training never decays — your factsheet is the script, and you can change it tomorrow.

Per-minute pricing punishes good calls

The perverse economics of per-minute billing: your best calls are your longest. A chatty caller booking a four-night family stay, a wedding planner walking through block details, a crew foreman with questions about weekly rates — under per-minute pricing, every one of them runs your meter. Under a flat retainer, the incentive points the right way: let the valuable call take the time it takes. No overage the month a tournament comes to town.

What you give up, honestly

An honest comparison cuts both ways. With Anchor you give up a human voice on first answer, and any task that requires an operator working live inside your systems. If your property needs concierge-level conversation on every ring, a staffed service may fit better — at staffed-service prices. What most limited-service properties actually need is never missing a booking call again, FAQs answered correctly at 1am, and a human reachable for the calls that matter. That's the trade.
