Rate guidance vs. rate quoting

The distinction that makes this safe: guidance is "our queens start at $109 depending on the night — let me get your dates and have the desk confirm your exact rate." Quoting is promising a number sight-unseen. Generic answering services either refuse to discuss money (and lose the caller) or quote wrong (and cost you margin). Ava gives the approved guidance you write into her factsheet, then captures and routes. Callers get a real answer; you keep rate authority.

The capture-and-confirm workflow

  1. Answer instantly, any hour, in the caller's language.
  2. Qualify: dates, nights, room type, party size, special requests, late arrival, reason for stay.
  3. Guide: availability posture and starting rates from your approved factsheet.
  4. Capture: name, number, email, and a callback commitment — flagged same-night when the caller is en route.
  5. Confirm: your team closes with the exact rate. During staffed hours that's typically a callback within minutes.

Group, crew, and wedding blocks

The highest-value reservation calls rarely book one room. Tournament coordinators, construction foremen, wedding planners, and travel nurses ask different questions and deserve different capture: room counts, date flexibility, rate expectations, decision dates. Ava collects the full picture and routes it to your group-sales contact — these are the leads that, missed once, fund a competitor's quarter. Direct bookings vs. OTA commissions

Every guest who calls is trying to book direct. When the call rings out, many of them book the same room through an OTA — and the commission comes out of your rate. Reservation call support is the unglamorous end of direct-booking strategy: pick up the phone every time, capture the lead every time. Speed-to-lead: why minutes matter

B2B sales teams have known the speed-to-lead numbers for years: contact a lead in the first minutes and conversion multiplies; wait a day and it craters. Hotel booking calls are the same dynamic compressed — the caller is comparing properties in real time, often with another tab or another number already open. That's why the workflow is built around immediacy twice over: the call itself is answered on the first ring at any hour, and captured leads carry a callback commitment your desk can actually honor. A reservation inquiry that gets a confirming call back within minutes feels, to the guest, indistinguishable from having reached you directly — because functionally they did.

What the desk sees

Every captured request arrives structured the same way: dates, nights, room type, party size, special requests, contact details, language, and an urgency flag. No voicemail transcription, no "call back a guy named Mike maybe about rooms." Your team works a queue of qualified, time-stamped leads — and the monthly report shows how many came in, when, and what they were worth pursuing.

Reservation call support FAQs

Does Ava quote rates?

She gives approved starting-rate guidance — "rooms start at $110, depending on availability" — and routes exact quotes and confirmations to your team. You control every confirmed number.

Can she take the reservation end to end?

She captures the complete request — dates, room type, party size, contact, special needs — and routes it for your team to confirm, typically with a fast callback. That's deliberately safer than giving an outside service booking authority.

How are group and block inquiries handled?

Group, crew, wedding, and team inquiries get extra capture: room counts, date ranges, reason for stay, decision timeline, and contact details, routed to whoever runs your group sales.

Does this help with OTA commissions?

Every direct booking call you capture is a booking that didn't need to come through a commissioned channel. Answering the phone is the cheapest direct-booking strategy a hotel has.

What hours does reservation support cover?

Whatever you forward: 24/7, after-hours only, or overflow when the desk doesn't answer. The flat monthly retainer is the same model either way.
