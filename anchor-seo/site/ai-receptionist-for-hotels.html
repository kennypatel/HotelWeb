Where it fits in daily operations

An AI receptionist isn't a department — it's a layer. During staffed hours it catches overflow so the desk can work the lobby; after hours it becomes the desk, answering from the factsheet and capturing what callers need; overnight it filters, waking your on-call contact for the lockout but not for the checkout-time question. The desk starts each morning with a structured report of everything that happened on the phone: questions answered, leads captured with full details, transfers made. Owners typically check it with coffee; the surprises fade after the first month and the captured leads don't.

What owners ask most

Two questions come up on nearly every call. "Will guests hate talking to an AI?" — some callers don't notice, some ask directly (she tells the truth), and the measurable thing is that answer rates go to one hundred percent while complaints don't materialize, because the alternative was voicemail. "What happens when she doesn't know?" — she says so and escalates or captures a callback, because an honest "let me have the desk call you right back" beats an invented answer every time.
